Philippine Corruption

An overview of public figures connected to corruption controversies, abuse of power, and major political scandals in the Philippines.

What is graft and corruption?

It is the misuse of power or position for personal gain, such as stealing public money, accepting bribes, or using government resources for personal benefit. It can harm the country, affect the lives of citizens, and reduce people's trust in the government.

01

Ferdinand Marcos Sr.

Former President · 1965–1986

Former President Ferdinand Marcos Sr. declared martial law in 1972. His rule is associated with authoritarianism, widespread human-rights violations, arrests, killings, and allegations of large-scale corruption.

His administration also left a legacy of heavy foreign debt and economic hardship.

Historical accounts of the Marcos era include documented abuses under martial law and efforts to recover ill-gotten wealth. Support specific claims with credible historical and government sources.

IM
02

Imelda Marcos

Former First Lady · 1965–1986

Imelda Marcos was the First Lady during Ferdinand Marcos Sr.'s presidency. After the People Power Revolution in 1986, she left the Philippines with her family and lived in exile in Hawaii.

After returning to the Philippines, she faced several corruption-related cases. For accuracy, distinguish allegations and charges from final court decisions.

JE
03

Joseph Estrada

13th President · 1998–2001

Joseph Estrada's presidency ended in 2001 during a political crisis following accusations of corruption and alleged links to illegal gambling payoffs.

He was tried for plunder and convicted in 2007, then granted executive clemency. Cite reliable sources when describing the case and its outcome.

GMA
04

Gloria Macapagal Arroyo

President · 2001–2010

Arroyo’s administration faced allegations of election manipulation connected to a leaked recording widely known as the “Hello Garci” controversy.

She was arrested in 2011 in connection with an electoral-sabotage case; the case was later dismissed by the court.

Arroyo served as vice president from 1998 to 2001 and president from 2001 to 2010. When describing legal controversies, include the final court outcome rather than presenting an accusation as a proven fact.

JSE
05

Jose “Jinggoy” Estrada

Senator · Public official

Jinggoy Estrada is a Filipino politician and senator, and the son of former president Joseph Estrada. He has faced corruption-related allegations, including cases connected to the pork-barrel fund controversy.

He was not a president of the Philippines; his political roles and the status of each case should be identified correctly.

Separate accusations from court findings and verify the latest status of proceedings before using this profile in a formal report.

Note on accuracy: This is an educational overview, not a legal record. Allegations, charges, convictions, appeals, and dismissals are different. Verify each case with reliable sources before submitting or publishing this material.

Useful references include official court decisions, the Presidential Commission on Good Government, the Commission on Audit, and established historical reporting.
